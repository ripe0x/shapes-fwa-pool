# Checkpoint timing and share pricing adversarial review

Date: 2026-11-23. Base: `e94fd99c072448bbbbb090c60d4f1e9b51df56ed` (`main`). Candidate: `codex/adversarial-checkpoint-nav`. This is an informed, bounded review of the pooled vault, not release approval. No mainnet transaction was sent.

## Answer and scope

**A purchaser-controlled keep can shift value to a newly priced depositor.** In the matched historical FWA replay below, a 1 ETH pending deposit priced before the keep receives **1,057,625,183,394,753 wei** more claim on final NAV than the same deposit priced after the keep. The incumbent dead-share seed loses exactly that amount. This is a real timing transfer in the share formula, but this depositor's claim remains below its entry payment before exit costs and gas. The fixture does not include a live incumbent holder, so a direct holder-to-holder realized profit is not established. At the production 10% allocation cap and the observed 0.06 ETH pack/1% FWA settlement fee, the *maximum gross keep revaluation* is 0.707869% of pre-flow NAV, before the depositor's share fraction. No contract change was made for this bounded timing exposure.

If FWA's owner reduces the settlement fee from 1% to zero, the corresponding cap rises to **0.816029% of pre-flow NAV** at the same pack backing and 10% exposure limit. The cap bounds aggregate revaluation, not an individual newcomer's fraction of it. It is independent of pool size, so hundreds of listings do not remove this economic timing gap.

**A cancellable deposit could block liquidation and delay an honest exit.** This was reproduced on the real fork before repair. The deposit was counted by `_need()` despite remaining refundable, so `liquidate()` returned `Nothing`. The attacker could cancel and checkpoint in one transaction, leaving the real idle pack unredeemed and consuming the 12-hour checkpoint slot. The repair excludes pending escrow from `_need()` and from the keeper's redemption shortfall calculation. The new fork regression failed with `Nothing` before the repair and passes afterwards; a keeper-policy regression schedules liquidation before checkpoint in the same state. This is a denial of service, not a demonstrated ETH theft.

**A canceled-charge carryover could subsidize an attacker's later deposit.** Two deposits shared a checkpoint charge; both canceled, leaving the first payer's 92,000,000,000,000 wei in `processingPool`. The next request quoted zero. The attacker's extra deposit and cancel consumed 198,513 measured call gas; at 0.2 gwei this is 39,702,600,000,000 wei before transaction overhead, below the charge saved on a planned later deposit. A payer now retains a protected pull-refund right if all requests cancel; the next request funds its own checkpoint. A gas-parameter increase with two charge contributors is covered by a second regression.

## Accounting model and rights

`_free = max(0, balance - pendingDepositTotal - redemptionReserve - distributionReserve - claimableTotal - processingPool - chargeRefundReserve)`. Checkpoint NAV is `_free + trackedValue + activeBacking + FWA feeCredit + bounded pendingFees`. The position book records pack redeem value and active backing separately. An allocation removes active backing from the book; a later bid returns the pack without a second backing loss. A keep removes the pack and returns `backing × (1 − settlement fee)` in cash. The [verified deployed FWAV2 source](https://repo.sourcify.dev/1/0x958C41181182e76F221331b2755b77D9e1426A98) matches runtime bytecode and confirms purchaser-only `keepNFT` and `acceptDepositorBid`, post-window depositor reclaim, permissionless finalization, a blackout gate on kicks, and forced ETH sends for keeps and kicks. The tests exercise those calls through the actual deployed FWA, Shapes, ShapePacks and coordinator.

| Step | Asset ownership and accounting transition |
|---|---|
| Deposit | ETH becomes refundable escrow; `pendingDepositTotal` excludes it from NAV. Checkpoint charge enters `processingPool` and is attributed to its payer; deployment charge remains in escrow until pricing. No shares yet. |
| Checkpoint and issuance | During FWA blackout with the acquisition queue drained, the vault harvests credit and proves bounded position state. It prices redemptions first, distributions second, then issues `net deposit × post-flow supply / post-flow NAV` shares. Deployment charges join NAV without issuing shares. |
| Redeem request | Shares move from liquid holder balance to `lockedShares`; the holder remains exposed to subsequent NAV until each fill. Checkpoint and unwind charges enter vault funds separately. |
| Partial or full fill | Available free ETH and escrow can fund a pro-rata fill at the checkpoint price. `redemptionReserve` is deducted from NAV; the unfilled fraction remains locked and exposed. `claim()` converts the holder's reserve entitlement to cash without spending free strategy ETH. |
| Fee accrual/collection | Active listing fees are priced through a bounded FWA pending-fee witness; selected fee credit is included directly. Harvest converts credit to cash and does not create a second gain. |
| Allocated pack | The pack's recorded redeem value stays in `trackedValue`; backing is removed from active backing. The purchaser owns a settlement choice, while FWA still holds the pack and backing. This is the timing gap tested below. |
| Keep / bid / expiry | Keep or silent finalization sends backing less fee to the vault and pack to purchaser. Bid sends discounted backing to purchaser and pack back to vault; no vault cash enters. A post-window reclaim can choose either outcome. `sync()` retires the booked position. |
| Kick / withdrawal | A kick needs FWA owner to remove ShapePacks' oracle exemption and a floor-cap breach; FWA refunds backing and returns pack. Ordinary withdrawal is the vault's own call. The book must reconcile them before NAV pricing. |
| Distribution | Only live shares receive the new gain above the high-water mark; reserve is excluded from NAV and later claimable. A priced new deposit gets future, not prior, distribution debt. |
| Cancellation / refund | If another holder flow remains, a canceled request's funded charge remains available for its checkpoint. If every flow cancels, residual contributions move to `chargeRefundReserve`, excluded from NAV, and each payer can claim their own amount. The next request must fund a new checkpoint. |
| Delay | A stale allocation past FWA's settlement window, unprocessed journal, pending receipt, or allocation exposure above the bound rejects checkpoint. Any caller may sync; each interval has one checkpoint slot. |

Expected invariants: (1) protected liabilities are never deployable; (2) `cash + packs + FWA claims − liabilities` matches NAV; (3) issuance buys only post-flow value; (4) all locked shares fill at the same pro-rata fraction per checkpoint; (5) a settlement is counted once, regardless of receive callback; (6) a cancellable deposit cannot be treated as certain liquidity for an independent liquidation decision; (7) a request cannot create a positive risk-free round trip after all charges, market positions and gas.

## Exact matched values and economic classification

At the replayed 0.06 ETH listing, backing is **64,896,174,863,380,000 wei**. A keep at FWA's 1% fee pays the vault **64,247,213,114,746,200 wei**, a **4,247,213,114,746,200 wei** increase over the recorded pack value. The real purchaser's historical acquisition request paid **70,375,794,939,344,277 wei**; that payment is common to both orderings, as is the 0.06 ETH pack received on keep. The vault paid two Shapes mint fees of 0.001 ETH each when building the pack, before the matched snapshot. Bob's gross entry payment is **1,000,072,000,000,000,000 wei**: 1 ETH escrow plus a 72,000,000,000,000 wei checkpoint charge. The deposit's deployment charge is **18,132,295,658,618,446 wei**. His remaining cash after entry is **999,928,000,000,000,000 wei** from a 2 ETH initial balance. Keeper receipts across the settlement and checkpoint are **122,000,000,000,000 wei** in both keep orderings. Bob's shares are marked at current NAV, not realized ETH; an exit would add withdrawal gas and any applicable charge. The after-pricing branch's marked claim is **12,603,559,921,811,663 wei below** the gross entry payment even before those costs. Purchaser ETH proceeds and pack ownership are identical in the matched keep branches, so the colluding pair's *incremental* marked claim is Bob's 1,057,625,183,394,753 wei; their total realized round-trip profit is not demonstrated.

The keep ordering is a **bounded transfer of a known future settlement gain** to newly priced shares. The bid ordering changes exposure to a real keeper cost; it does not count backing twice. A redeeming holder's unfilled shares retain exposure, while paid shares become a protected liability. A purchaser or withdrawing holder cannot force a bid loss *after* pricing because allocation already removed backing from NAV. This is a source/accounting argument backed by the bid replay, not an exhaustive search over all holdings.

For a deposit-only, immediate mark after a controlled keep, let `N` be pre-deposit NAV, `D` the escrow, `C` its deployment charge and `G` the keep uplift. The new holder's marked value less escrow has numerator `(D − C)G − CN` over positive denominator `N + D − C`. At current two paid Shapes mints, `C/(D − C)` is at least about **1.60256%** because the mint fees alone are 0.002 ETH per 0.1248 ETH reference position. The allocated 10% bound limits `G/N` to **0.816029%** even with a zero FWA settlement fee. Thus a deposit made solely to capture a bounded keep cannot clear its deployment charge, before checkpoint charge, purchaser costs, exit costs or gas. This inequality assumes the current mint fee, one 0.06 ETH recipe, no other gain or loss, and distribution off; it does not establish a full-cycle no-profit theorem for every colluding position.

The cancellation finding is **denial of service**. Its correction can redeem an idle pack before a pending deposit is finally priced, causing avoidable conversion and, when an essential budget exists, real keeper pay. The production values set essential budget to zero, so that liquidation pays no keeper from vault funds. The fixed test's fork parameters retain a funded essential budget and measured one-pack pay; the economic tradeoff should be judged under both settings. The charge-carryover finding is **request-cost transfer** with a maximum one checkpoint quote per idle interval. The refund reserve removes the transfer; it adds a contribution storage write per charged request and a payer-funded refund claim when all requests cancel. No extra keeper payment or duplicated charge was observed.

### Things that don't make sense

- The one-hour allocation window can overlap the last 15 minutes of a purchase interval, but a checkpoint still prices the pack at its redeemable value when the purchaser can choose a keep worth more. The exposure bound limits the shift; it does not make the two orderings economically identical.
- A canceled sponsor must make a separate claim after every request cancels. At high gas, the gas for a small refund may outweigh that refund until fees fall.
- The production essential budget is zero, so a holder or volunteer must pay gas to liquidate even after the liveness fix.
- Existing forced-kick recovery tests replace the vault's receiver code to induce failure; they do not establish that the original receiver fails within FWA's 100,000 gas stipend.
